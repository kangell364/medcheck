# TIC Chapter 1806 — rebating and unjust discrimination: examinable provisions

Source: Texas Insurance Code Chapter 1806, statutes.capitol.texas.gov, read
15 September 2026. Section numbers are exact; quoted phrases are verbatim
from `IN.1806.txt` in this directory.

This is the chapter the exam blueprint's second citation for *Rebating*
points at, and it is the live one. See "Where rebating actually lives" below.

## The structural point a candidate has to get right first

Chapter 1806 does not state one rebating rule. It states the **same rule
three times, once per line of business**, in three subchapters with three
different section numbers:

| Subchapter | Lines covered | Prohibition | $25 item allowed by |
| ---------- | ------------- | ----------- | ------------------- |
| B (§§1806.051–.059) | Automobile | **§1806.053**, **§1806.054** | §1806.059 |
| C (§§1806.101–.107) | Casualty; fidelity, guaranty and surety bonds | **§1806.104** | §1806.1041 |
| D (§§1806.151–.156) | Fire and allied lines | **§1806.153** | §1806.1541 |

A question that asks "under which section" is testing whether the candidate
noticed the line of business. A question that asks what the rule *is* can be
answered from any of the three, because they say the same thing.

**§1806.102(c)** is worth reading once: Subchapter C excludes automobile,
life/health/accident, professional liability, reinsurance, aircraft,
fraternal, fire, workers' compensation, marine, title, and most weather,
flood, earthquake, vandalism and sprinkler-leakage coverage. Casualty here
means liability and bonds, not "everything that is not life".

## What is prohibited

**§1806.104(b)** (casualty) — an insurer, an insurer's employee, or a broker
or agent may not "directly or indirectly pay, allow, or give, or offer to
pay, allow, or give, as an inducement to insurance, or after insurance has
been written, a rebate, discount, abatement, credit or reduction of the
premium stated in an insurance policy, or a special favor or advantage in the
dividends or other benefits to accrue on the policy, or any valuable
consideration or inducement, not specified in the policy."

Four things in that sentence are each independently testable:

1. **"directly or indirectly"** — routing the benefit through a third party
   does not save it.
2. **"or after insurance has been written"** — a rebate is still a rebate if
   it is paid at renewal or as a thank-you. It does not have to induce the
   sale.
3. **"not specified in the policy"** — this is the whole test. A discount
   that appears in the filed policy is a rate; the identical money handed
   over outside the policy is a rebate.
4. **"any valuable consideration"** — not just cash. Anything of value.

**§1806.053(1)** (automobile) adds the discrimination half: an insurer may
not "discriminate or make a distinction... among insureds having like
hazards". *Like hazards* is the operative phrase — charging different
premiums for different risks is the business; charging different premiums for
the same risk is unjust discrimination.

**§1806.104(a)** — separately from rebating, it is prohibited to knowingly
issue a policy, or charge a premium, "that is not in accordance with an
applicable filing". Deviating from the filed rate in the insured's favour is
rebating; deviating either way is this.

## Accepting a rebate is also prohibited — and can be a crime

**§1806.104(c)** — "An insured named in an insurance policy or an employee of
an insured may not knowingly receive or accept, directly or indirectly, a
rebate..." The insured is a violator too.

**§1806.156** (fire and allied lines) goes further and makes acceptance a
criminal offence:

> "(a) A person commits an offense if the person knowingly receives or
> accepts... a rebate of premium payable on an insurance policy..."
>
> "(b) An offense under this section is punishable by: (1) a fine of not more
> than $100; (2) confinement in jail for not more than 90 days; or (3) both a
> fine and confinement."

$100 / 90 days is a specific, quotable number and a plausible exam answer.
Note the asymmetry: the criminal penalty sits in the **fire** subchapter
only.

## What is allowed

**The $25 item.** §§1806.059, 1806.1041 and 1806.1541 (all added by S.B. 840,
83rd Leg., effective 1 September 2013) permit "an item that is a promotional
advertising item, educational item, or traditional courtesy commonly extended
to consumers and that is valued at $25 or less."

Three constraints hide in that phrase: the item must fall into one of the
three named kinds, it must be valued at **$25 or less**, and the ceiling is on
the *item*, not on a per-year total.

**Loss-control services — §1806.002**, added by H.B. 1074 (88th Leg.,
effective 1 September 2023) and the newest rule in the chapter. An insurer or
agent may offer services "not specified in the insurance policy that relate to
loss control of the risks covered under the policy" for free or at a discount,
provided:

- the offer is "not unfairly discriminatory to insureds or applicants of the
  same class and of essentially the same hazard";
- **§1806.002(b)** the cost to the insurer "must be reasonable in comparison
  to that customer's premiums or insurance coverage for the policy class" —
  so there is no fixed dollar cap here, unlike the $25 item; and
- **§1806.002(c)** the insurer may not give any data obtained about the
  customer through the service to another person for any purpose other than
  loss control.

That last clause is the modern one: a free telematics device or leak sensor is
legal, and selling what it records is not.

**Profit sharing and policyholder dividends** — §§1806.055, 1806.105 (general),
1806.056 (combat duty), 1806.057 and 1806.106 (nonprofit business
associations), 1806.154 (fire). The recurring conditions:

- the profit-sharing agreement must be **contained in the policy** (§1806.105(a);
  §1806.154(a)(1) says "on or in the face of the policy");
- distribution must be **uniform** and may not discriminate among insureds of
  the same class;
- nothing may be distributed **before the policy expires**;
- the insurer must have adequate reserves; and
- **the 10 percent rule** — a policyholder dividend amount **exceeding 10
  percent of surplus** needs the commissioner's approval; at or below 10
  percent the insurer need only notify the commissioner in writing
  (§1806.105(c)–(d), §1806.058(b)–(c), §1806.056(b), §1806.106(b)).
- **Five business days** — where an application is required, if the
  commissioner does not act on it "on or before the fifth business day after
  the date the commissioner receives the application, the distribution is
  considered approved" (§1806.056(c), §1806.057(c), §1806.106(c)).

**Filed rating plans** are not rebating: §1806.052 and §1806.103(b) preserve
rate modification under a plan filed under Chapter 2251 (or Article 5.13-2)
that the commissioner has not disapproved. This is the statutory basis for
the distinction taught as "a filed discount is a rate, an unfiled one is a
rebate."

## Consequences

**§1806.107(a)** — "A violation of this subchapter is unjust discrimination
and rebating." **§1806.107(b)** — the commissioner "may revoke the certificate
of authority of an insurer that violates this subchapter or the license of an
agent who violates this subchapter." §1806.055(c)–(d) says the same for
automobile. The fire subchapter is narrower: §1806.153(c) and §1806.154(b)
declare only that a violator "has engaged in unjust discrimination", and
attach no revocation clause of their own — the criminal penalty in
§1806.156 is the fire subchapter's distinctive sanction.

Note what the remedy is: **revocation**, by the commissioner. Chapter 1806
carries no private right of action and no damages provision. A question that
offers "the insured may sue for treble damages" as an answer to a bare
rebating fact pattern is reaching for Chapter 541, which is a different
chapter with different elements.

## Where rebating actually lives

Publication #124401 (effective 1 September 2026) cites **TIC 541.056** for
*Rebating*. That section does not exist — chapter 541 runs .051, .052, .053,
.054, .055, then .059, and the same gap appears for .057 and .058. Verified
again against the re-extracted `IN.541.txt` on 15 September 2026.

The blueprint's second citation, **TIC 1806.104**, is correct and current.
Teach rebating from Chapter 1806. The gap recorded in `README.md` is closed.

The general lesson stands and is now confirmed twice over: **the blueprint is
authoritative about what is examined and unreliable about where the law
sits.** Check every citation against the text.
